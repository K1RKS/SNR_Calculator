Notes

  • Path model. This calculator uses Friis free-space path loss only. It does not account for terrain, trees, buildings, Earth curvature, or Fresnel clearance. Treat results as an ideal upper bound for planning.
  • SNR reference lines. Higher SNR supports higher modulation and more reliable links. The dashed lines are common Wi‑Fi / mesh planning rules of thumb (exact MCS thresholds vary by radio and channel width):
    • 20 dB — comfortable / “good” link; usually supports solid mid-to-high rates with margin for fade.
    • 15 dB — usable / “OK” link; often workable for moderate throughput, less headroom.
    • 10 dB — marginal; may stay associated at low rates but is sensitive to noise and fade.
    • 5 dB — very weak; often dropouts or failure under real conditions.
    Where your curve crosses these lines is a quick estimate of usable distance each way.
  • Channel width. Changing channel width adjusts both noise floors by 10·log10(new/old) to reflect thermal noise scaling (about +3 dB when doubling bandwidth).
